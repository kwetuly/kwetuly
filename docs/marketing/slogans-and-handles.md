# Rafrika: slogan options and social handle plan

Status: drafts only, 2026-10-24. Walter chooses. Nothing here is public yet.

Brand confirmed by Walter on 2026-10-24: Rafrika (rafiki = friend + Afrika). Guide-first travel platform: travellers find and talk directly to local guides, book guide packages, prep calls and group trips, and find listed businesses (stays, transport, shops). Starting in Tanzania, later all of Africa.

Rules used for every line below: no numbers, ratings or invented facts; no "verified", "best", "#1", "trusted" or similar claims; sentence case (no all caps); each line works beyond Tanzania, with a Tanzania variant where useful.

Kiswahili check needed: the Kiswahili lines below are drafts. The `localization` agent and a native speaker should confirm tone and wording before anything is used.

## Five slogan options

### 1. Travel with a friend who lives here.

- Kiswahili: Safiri na rafiki anayeishi hapa.
- Why it works: it explains the name (rafiki = friend) without spelling it out, and "here" moves with the traveller, so it works in any country we open.
- Where it fits: website hero headline, app store subtitle.

### 2. Meet your guide before you go.

- Kiswahili: Mfahamu kiongozi wako kabla ya safari.
- Why it works: it describes exactly what is different about Rafrika (profiles, messages and prep calls before booking) and promises nothing we cannot show.
- Where it fits: app store subtitle, ad headline, social bio first line.

### 3. A trip is only as good as your guide. So choose yours.

- Tanzania variant (current line): Tanzania is only as good as your guide. So choose yours.
- Kiswahili: Safari ni nzuri kama kiongozi wako. Basi, mchague mwenyewe.
- Kiswahili, Tanzania variant: Tanzania ni nzuri kama kiongozi wako. Basi, mchague mwenyewe.
- Why it works: it keeps the line we already use, puts the choice in the traveller's hands, and the general version carries over to every new country.
- Where it fits: website hero (long form), launch campaign, about page. Too long for most app store subtitles (30 characters on the App Store).

### 4. Every journey starts with a rafiki.

- Kiswahili: Kila safari huanza na rafiki.
- Why it works: it teaches the word "rafiki" to people who do not speak Kiswahili and ties it straight to the brand name; it says nothing about place, so it scales across Africa.
- Where it fits: social bio, video end card, merchandise and guide T-shirts, footer.

### 5. Find your guide. Talk directly. Travel your way.

- Kiswahili: Mpate kiongozi wako. Ongea naye moja kwa moja. Safiri upendavyo.
- Why it works: three short steps that match how the product actually works (browse guides, message them, plan the trip together) and stay true in any country.
- Where it fits: website hero sub-headline under option 1 or 3, app store description first line, onboarding screen.

### Marketing recommendation (Walter decides)

- Main line: option 1 (warm, explains the name, travels well).
- Supporting line: option 2 for app stores and ads, because it says what the product does in a few words.
- Keep option 3 (Tanzania variant) for the Tanzania launch campaign, since it is already in use.

Before using any line publicly: `legal` should do a quick check that no other travel company uses the same line in our markets.

## Social handle plan

Availability has not been checked. Walter (or someone he names) should check each platform by hand and register the handles he approves. Nobody should contact owners of taken handles without Walter.

### Order of preference

1. `@rafrika` on every platform. First choice: shortest, same as the brand.
2. `@rafrika.africa`: says where we operate and scales beyond Tanzania. On X dots are not allowed, so use `@rafrika_africa` there.
3. `@rafrikaapp`: clear, but the double "a" is easy to misread. If dots are allowed, `@rafrika.app` reads better.
4. `@gorafrika`: short, but can be read as "gora frika". Use only if the options above are taken.
5. Extra fallback idea: `@rafrikatravel` (Instagram/TikTok can use `@rafrika.travel`).

Rule: aim for the same handle on all platforms. If `@rafrika` is free on some platforms but not others, it may be better to use one fallback everywhere instead of mixing. Walter decides once availability is known.
