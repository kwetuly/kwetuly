# Kwetuly: slogan options and social handle plan

Status: drafts only, 2026-10-24. Walter chooses. Nothing here is public yet, and no handle or account has been claimed.

Brand: Kwetuly (was Rafrika). Walter bought kwetuly.com on 2026-10-24. This replaces the earlier Rafrika slogan and handle plan, so `@rafrika` handles and "rafiki" lines are no longer used.

- Meaning: "kwetu" is Kiswahili for "our place, our home", plus "-ly".
- Say it: kweh-TOO-lee.
- What it is: a guide-first travel platform. Travellers meet and choose a local guide first, then book one-on-one trips, cultural day trips, prep calls and group trips, and find listed businesses. Starting in Tanzania, aiming for all of Africa.

Rules used for every line below:
- No numbers, ratings or invented facts.
- No "verified", "best", "#1", "trusted" or similar claims.
- Sentence case (no all caps).
- Lines must stay true while we only operate in Tanzania. Any line that mentions Africa is marked, and has a Tanzania version.

Kiswahili check needed: all Kiswahili lines are drafts. The `localization` agent and a native speaker should confirm tone and wording before anything is used. Two words in particular need checking:
- "kiongozi" (leader or guide), compared with "mwongozaji" or "kiongozi wa watalii".
- "mwenyeji" (local resident or host). "Host" might suggest that we offer homestays.

## Slogans: five in English

### E1. Welcome to our place.
- Why: this is "karibu kwetu" in English, so it explains the name directly. It is warm and short, and it works in any country.
- Where: website hero, video end card, social bio first line.

### E2. Our place. Your guide.
- Why: it puts the name ("our place") next to what makes us different (you choose the guide). It is short enough for an App Store subtitle (30 characters).
- Where: app store subtitle, ad headline.

### E3. Travel with someone who calls it home.
- Why: our guides live where they work, and this line says that without claiming anything we can't show. It works in any country.
- Where: website sub-headline, guide-recruiting posts (it treats guides with respect).

### E4. Choose your guide. Come to our place.
- Why: two steps that match how the product actually works: meet and choose a guide first, then travel.
- Where: onboarding screen, ads aimed at independent travellers.

### E5. Tanzania, from the people who call it home.
- Later version: "Africa, from the people who call it home." Use this only once we have live guides in more than one country. Until then it would overstate what we offer.
- Why: it connects the place to the people who live there and is kind to communities.
- Where: Tanzania launch campaign, places pages, press.

The current line, "Tanzania is only as good as your guide. So choose yours.", still fits the product. Marketing suggests keeping it as the Tanzania campaign line next to the new brand line.

## Slogans: five in Kiswahili

### K1. Karibu kwetu.
- Meaning: Welcome to our place / our home.
- Why: Tanzanians already say this every day, and it contains the brand name. Visitors learn "karibu" on their first day, so they will recognise it.

### K2. Kwetu ni kwako.
- Meaning: Our place is your place.
- Why: hospitable and short. It turns the name into an invitation.

### K3. Safiri na wenyeji.
- Meaning: Travel with local people.
- Why: it says plainly that the guides are local. The native speaker should confirm that "wenyeji" reads as "local people" and not as "hosts who give you a bed".

### K4. Mchague kiongozi wako, karibu kwetu.
- Meaning: Choose your guide, welcome to our place.
- Why: the Kiswahili partner of E4. It keeps the "choose yours" idea from the current line.

### K5. Kiongozi wako, mwenyeji wako.
- Meaning: Your guide, your local (person who knows the place).
- Why: rhythmic and easy to remember. Check that "mwenyeji" does not suggest lodging.

## Top pick (Walter decides)

**"Welcome to our place." in English, with "Karibu kwetu." in Kiswahili.**

Why:
1. It is the meaning of the name, so it teaches people the brand every time it is used, and it gives the pronunciation a natural home ("karibu kwetu" leads into "Kwetuly").
2. It is true today and stays true in every country we open. It promises no feature, number or quality claim.
3. "Karibu" is the one Kiswahili word most visitors already know, so the line works for travellers and for Tanzanian guides alike.
4. Kind to communities: it works best when guides say it in their own voice (videos, guide profiles). That way "our place" means the guides' and communities' home, not a company claiming Africa. Marketing recommends guides deliver it on camera, with their consent, rather than using it only as a corporate tagline.

Supporting line: E2, "Our place. Your guide.", for app stores and ads, because it also says what the product does.

Before using any line publicly, `legal` should do a quick check that no travel company in our markets uses the same line. "Karibu kwetu" is an everyday phrase, so we can use it but probably cannot own it as a trademark.
