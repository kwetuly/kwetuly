## 1. Lipa kidogo kidogo (A3)

### 1.1 The shape Walter's plan recommends

Shape (a) from the ideas file, written as product rules so the advocate can test each one:

1. Only toward **one trip that is already booked** (an accepted offer, package or group-trip place) with a known total price in TZS.
2. A **fixed schedule** agreed at booking: number of parts, amounts and due dates. The last part is due **before the 8-day cut-off**.
3. **No wallet, no balance, no credit.** Money paid toward trip X can only ever go to trip X, or back to the traveller as a refund. It cannot be moved to another trip, another person or another guide, and cannot be spent anywhere else.
4. **The trip happens only after the last part is paid.** Kwetuly and the guide never let a traveller travel first and pay later.
5. **No interest, no plan fee and no late fee** at the start.
6. Each traveller pays his or her own share. **No pooled group savings** (for example a group of friends paying into one pot).
7. Refund rules are the published deposit and refund rules ("who causes it pays", up to 10% kept on traveller cancellations, working number), applied to the total actually paid.

Shape (b), an open savings wallet, and shape (c), a savings product run by a licensed bank or mobile-money partner, are described in the ideas file. Shape (b) is not recommended (section 1.3). Shape (c) is a partner's product and only Walter would approach a partner.

### 1.3 The risk, in plain words

My reading, for the advocate to confirm or correct [Unverified]:

- **The biggest risk is the one deposits already have, made longer.** If parts are paid into Kwetuly's own Selcom merchant account and kept there for weeks or months before reaching the guide, Kwetuly is holding travellers' money for others for longer. That is the open payments-structure question (lessons-payments-items-research.md section 2.3, options A to D), and instalments make it heavier, not different.
- **Shape (a) looks less like e-money than a wallet.** As usually described, e-money is stored value that can be used to pay other people and can be redeemed. Under shape (a) the money cannot be spent anywhere, cannot be moved, and goes only to one named trip or back as a refund. So it reads more like **part-payments of one contract price**. Whether Tanzanian law sees it that way is the first advocate question.
- **Shape (a) looks less like deposit-taking than savings.** The reported "banking business" definition is about taking funds from the public and **using them for lending or investment**. Kwetuly would do neither. But the "deposits payable upon demand" wording is a worry if travellers can ask for their money back at any time. Refunds under fixed cancellation rules may be different from "payable on demand". Advocate to say.
- **Shape (b), an open savings wallet, is the high-risk version.** Stored value, open-ended, usable later for any trip: this is close to e-money or deposit-taking, and if the 2020 circular is still in force, a non-mobile-operator company may not even be able to get an e-money licence. This matches the parked credits idea on the board.
- **Travel first, pay later would be credit.** If a traveller could travel before paying in full, Kwetuly or the guide would be giving credit. Digital lenders are Tier 2 under the Microfinance Act and need a BoT licence. Rule 4 in section 1.1 avoids this. **Late fees or a plan fee** could also be read as a credit charge or an unfair penalty; rule 5 avoids this until the advocate answers.
- **Pooled group saving** (friends paying into one pot) may look like a community savings group or collective scheme. Rule 6 avoids it.
- **Tourism licensing.** Collecting money for trips over months strengthens the argument that Kwetuly sells trips, which could need an MNRT licence (paid-checks-operators-crews.md section 2.1).
- **Consumer law.** Each part payment, the schedule, the total, provider fees per part, what happens on a missed part and the refund on each kind of cancellation must be clear before the first payment. A rule that keeps everything paid after one missed part is likely unfair.
- **Fees.** Each small payment carries provider fees (to verify with Selcom, Walter's contact). Passing them to travellers must be disclosed; the card-surcharge question in the ideas file A1 applies.

### 1.4 Safe interim shape (until the advocate answers)

1. **Nothing goes live and nothing is announced.** No "pay little by little" text on the website or app.
2. If Walter wants a test before the advocate answers, the safest version is **"a schedule on paper, paid directly"**: the guide or operator offers its own instalment plan; the booking shows the agreed schedule; the traveller pays each part **directly to the guide or operator**; Kwetuly only shows the schedule and sends reminders. Kwetuly holds no money. The booking page says plainly: "You pay [guide or operator name] directly. Kwetuly does not hold this money."
3. When payments are live and the advocate has chosen a structure, the parts follow the **same structure as deposits**, preferably a licensed provider holding the funds (option A), never a Kwetuly-held balance.
4. Draft default rules for a missed part (for the advocate to test, numbers are placeholders):
   - a reminder before each due date and a grace period of [7] days after it;
   - if a part is still unpaid after the grace period, the traveller is asked to pay or cancel; the booking is cancelled only after a written notice;
   - on cancellation for a missed part, the normal traveller-cancellation rule applies to the total paid (up to [10]% kept, the rest refunded);
   - **guide or operator cancels**: 100% of every part paid is refunded;
   - **trip cannot run** for reasons outside anyone's control: the force majeure rule in the deposit policy applies;
   - the last part is due before the 8-day cut-off; if it is missed, the same rule as above applies.
5. No interest, no plan fee, no late fee, no group pot, no transfer of paid parts to another trip.

### 1.5 Draft wording (English; Kiswahili and the 7 traveller languages only after review)

> **Pay in parts for this trip**
> Total price: TZS [amount]. You pay in [3] parts: TZS [a] by [date], TZS [b] by [date], TZS [c] by [date]. The last part is due 8 days before your trip starts.
> This money is only for this trip. It is not savings and cannot be used for anything else.
> If you cancel, or miss a part and do not pay within [7] days of our reminder, our refund rules apply: [link]. If [guide name] cancels, you get back everything you paid.
> Each payment may carry a provider fee of [amount], shown before you pay.

### 2.2 The risk, in plain words

**I1, money donations.** My reading [Unverified]:
- A company collecting gifts from the public for third parties combines the payments question, a possible fundraising or charity question nobody has answered, accounting and tax questions, and a strong honesty risk. It is the highest-risk item in this note.
- The ideas file already says it must never be hidden income. Even with a zero cut, Kwetuly would need to show where every shilling went.
- The cleaner route, if Walter wants donations at all, is a **separately registered body** (I4, the foundation) with its own books, or pointing donors to an existing registered organisation. Pointing donors to an outside organisation is an endorsement question in its own right: Kwetuly must not suggest it has checked an organisation it has not checked.

**I3, porter hardship notices.** My reading [Unverified]:
- A notice that a porter is ill, injured, bereaved or in debt will usually contain **health or family information, which is sensitive data**, and often other people's data (a sick child, a widow). Prior written consent of each person, or of a guardian for a child, is the reported rule.
- Many porters use basic phones. How a porter gives **written** consent (a signed paper form? an SMS? a recorded voice message?) is an open question.
- **Power imbalance.** A guide or operator posting about "his" porter may pressure the porter to agree. Consent from the porter, not the guide, is needed (the same rule as C2).
- **Dignity.** The voice guide forbids pity wording. A public notice can follow a porter for years, for example when looking for work.
- **Defamation.** A notice that blames an operator or another person for the hardship could be defamatory.
- **Money.** A notice that asks for money is a donation appeal and falls under I1.

### 2.3 Safe interim shape

1. **I1: park.** No money donations through Kwetuly, no donate button, no bank or mobile-money number for donations shown on Kwetuly, until the company is registered, the advocate has answered and Walter has decided whether a separate body (I4) is needed. This matches the ideas file recommendation (decision 6).
2. **I2 gear donations** stay on the 2026-11-06 paper plan (goods only, no money).
3. **I3: park public notices.** If Walter later approves a pilot after the advocate answers, draft rules:
   - the porter asks for it, not the guide; the porter's own written consent in Kiswahili, with what will be shown, where, for how long, and how to remove it;
   - no health details, no diagnosis, no photos of injuries, no children, no other family members named;
   - the porter approves the exact wording before it is shown, and can remove it at any time with one message, free;
   - no request for money until I1 is answered; no names of operators or others blamed;
   - shown only for a fixed period, for example [30] days, then removed;
   - consent records kept; the notice itself deleted when removed.

### 3.2 The risk, in plain words

My reading [Unverified]:
- **In-person cooking classes are the higher-risk class.** Food is prepared and eaten; there are knives, fire, hot oil, sometimes a market visit, sometimes a home visit. Food poisoning, allergic reactions, burns and safety on home visits are real risks. A class in a home may need a food premises permit or health certificate if it counts as a food business; nobody has confirmed this.
- **Kwetuly's role.** If Kwetuly approves cooks, sets the format or shows "checked", a traveller harmed in a class may name Kwetuly. The badge rule protects only if it says exactly what was checked.
- **Allergy data.** Collecting it in Kwetuly's forms makes Kwetuly a holder of sensitive data. Not collecting it and leaving it all to the traveller may be unsafe. The middle path is below.
- **Recording.** Recording without everyone's agreement, or catching a child on camera, breaks the consent rules. Re-using clips for marketing needs separate consent.
- **Children.** In-person cooking classes are often family activities. Adults-only stays the rule for now; a family version needs its own rules later (section 3.3).
- **Tax.** While the traveller pays the cook directly (in-person, until payments are live), Kwetuly pays no one and the withholding question does not arise; it returns when Kwetuly pays teachers out.
- **Online classes.** As in the earlier note: no school registration rule found for adult private tutors; TCRA online content question; start with teachers who live in Tanzania.

### 3.3 Safe interim shape

**Kiswahili lessons (B1)**
1. Adults only; learners confirm they are 18 or over; teachers end the lesson and report if a learner seems to be a minor (already drafted in the earlier note and docs/team/teacher-code-of-conduct.md).
2. Teachers living in Tanzania only at first.
3. No recording by default. Recording only if both sides tick "yes" before each lesson; never used for marketing without a separate consent; deleted after [30] days unless the learner asks to keep it.
4. Profile shows only what Kwetuly checked, and when (for example "BAKITA certificate seen 12 Nov 2026"). Nothing else is called "certified".
5. Online sales wait for live payments and the advocate's payment structure.

**Cooking classes (B2), in person first**
1. **Not shown publicly until the advocate answers the permit question** (section 3.4, questions 17 to 19). Paper design and cook profiles can be prepared.
2. Adults only. The listing says so.
3. The cook declares, and the listing shows as **"declared by the cook"** unless Kwetuly saw a document:
   - where the class takes place (home, restaurant, outdoor);
   - any food premises permit or health certificate the cook holds (number and date if seen);
   - group size, length, whether there is a market visit;
   - every ingredient in each dish, including common allergens (nuts, sesame, gluten, milk, eggs, fish, shellfish);
   - whether alcohol is served (if yes, it needs its own advocate check).
4. **Allergies and diet:** the listing shows the ingredients; the traveller is asked, before booking, to tell the cook of any allergy or diet. Kwetuly does not store allergy data in a profile field. If a booking form asks for it, it is optional, has a clear written consent line, is seen only by the cook for that class and is deleted [7] days after the class (security-reviewer to check the design).
5. No health or nutrition claims ("healthy", "cures", "good for diabetes") in any listing.
6. Home visits: the booking shows the cook's name and the area before payment; the exact address is shared only after booking; the traveller can share the booking with a friend. Kwetuly never promises safety.
7. The traveller pays the cook directly on the day; the listing says so; any Kwetuly commission or fee is disclosed (Kwetuly rule).
8. The cook does not sell or arrange insurance through Kwetuly; Kwetuly does not either.
9. No recording or photos of the cook's family or home without their agreement; children never shown.

**Online cooking classes** follow the Kiswahili rules above, plus the ingredient list sent before the class.
