## 1. Crypto (parked): background only

### 1.1 Bank of Tanzania (BoT) position

- **2019 public notice and later reminder.** BoT warned the public against trading, marketing and using virtual currencies. It said the Tanzanian shilling is the only legal tender and relied on sections 26 and 27 of the Bank of Tanzania Act, 2006. A later reminder said using virtual currencies breaks the law. [Official, copies on bot.go.tz: [notice 1](https://www.bot.go.tz/Adverts/PressRelease/en/2020031307240424208.pdf), [notice 2](https://www.bot.go.tz/Adverts/PressRelease/en/2020031307240426214.pdf). I have not confirmed which file is which, or whether either has been withdrawn.] Commentary: [Bowmans, "The ban on cryptocurrency in Tanzania"](https://bowmanslaw.com/insights/the-ban-on-cryptocurrency-in-tanzania/) [Secondary].
- **Payment providers.** BoT's National Payment System annual report for 2024 is reported to say that licensed payment system providers may not issue, distribute or settle virtual assets. [Secondary report of an official document. Unverified: I could not open the report.] So a licensed Tanzanian payment partner probably cannot process crypto for Kwetuly.
- **Regulation in progress.** BoT has finished a "regulatory concept" for virtual assets ([The Citizen, 2025](https://www.thecitizen.co.tz/tanzania/business/bot-completes-regulatory-concept-framework-for-crypto-regulation-5540320)) [Secondary]. In July 2026 the Governor was reported as saying that laws and regulations on virtual assets, cryptocurrencies and stablecoins are being finalised. No date has been given ([Bitcoin.com News, July 2026](https://news.bitcoin.com/bank-of-tanzania-targets-crypto-as-governor-warns-of-terror-finance-risks/); [Tanzania Insight](https://tanzaniainsight.com/bot-finalising-crypto-regulations/)) [Secondary]. Crypto-industry news has also reported a sandbox test of a TZS stablecoin. [Unverified]
- **Court.** In *Yellow Card Tanzania Limited v. Nyamwero Michael Nyamwero*, Commercial Case No. 12171 of 2024, decided 13 December 2024, the High Court is reported to have held that trading cryptocurrency is not illegal and enforced a settlement deed ([Afriwise, citing Bowmans](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/tanzania-courts-decision-on-virtual-assets)) [Secondary. I have not read the judgment.] It is a single High Court decision between private parties. It does not change BoT's notice or the rules for payment providers.

### 1.2 Tax on digital-asset transactions (Finance Act 2024)

- The Finance Act 2024 added **section 83C to the Income Tax Act (Cap. 332)**. It is reported to require anyone, resident or not, who owns a digital-asset exchange platform or **facilitates** the exchange or transfer of digital assets to withhold **3%** of payments made to residents for that exchange or transfer ([TRA page on digital taxes](https://www.tra.go.tz/page/digital-service-tax) [Official, but I could not load the page directly. The wording comes from a search summary]; [PwC Tanzania, Finance Act 2024 alert](https://www.pwc.co.tz/assets/pdf/pwc-tanzania-finance-act-update-2024.pdf) [Secondary]).
- "Digital asset" is reported to mean intangible items of value such as cryptocurrencies and tokens generated and held by cryptographic methods ([Fintech Magazine Africa](https://fintechmagazine.africa/2024/06/25/tanzania-unveils-3-tax-on-digital-asset-transactions-in-2024-25-budget/)) [Secondary].
- Reported to apply from 1 July 2024. [Unverified]
- **Not to be confused with** the Digital Services Tax on non-resident digital service providers (reported to have risen from 2% to 3% from 1 July 2026 [Unverified]), or the 5% withholding on payments to resident digital content creators (s. 83B, see section 3.2).

### 1.3 Could Kwetuly ever accept or pay out crypto legally?

My reading, for the advocate to confirm or correct: **not now, and probably not until BoT publishes its framework.**
1. **Accepting crypto from travellers.** Licensed payment providers reportedly cannot settle it. BoT's notice is still on its site. The Foreign Currency Usage Regulations, 2025 (see section 2.4) require goods and services in Tanzania to be priced and paid in TZS, apart from listed exceptions. Taking crypto for services delivered in Tanzania looks hard to fit in. [Unverified]
2. **Paying guides or teachers in crypto.** Same problems. Kwetuly would also probably count as "facilitating transfers" under s. 83C, so it would have to withhold 3%. [Unverified]
3. **A route that might work later.** Crypto is handled entirely by a foreign provider that is licensed where it operates, and it converts to fiat before any money reaches Tanzania. Kwetuly would only ever receive and pay out fiat. Even this needs the advocate's view on BoT's notice, foreign-exchange rules and s. 83C.

**Recommendation:** keep crypto parked. Ask the advocate to tell us when BoT's virtual-asset rules are published.

## 2. Collecting money for others (feature b)

### 2.1 The rules that may apply

- **National Payment Systems Act, 2015** (now Cap. 437, R.E. 2023). Reported wording: "A person shall not operate a payment system without a payment system licence issued by the Bank." Banks need BoT approval; non-banks need a licence. ([Act, Act Supplement No. 4 of 2015](https://commons.laws.africa/akn/tz/act/2015/4/media/publication/tz-act-2015-4-publication-document.pdf) [Official copy]; [Cap. 437 R.E. 2023, copy hosted by a law firm](https://www.vkadvocates.co.tz/LIBRARY/Cap.%20437%20National%20Payment%20System%20Act,%20R.E%202023.pdf) [Secondary host])
- **Payment Systems (Licensing and Approval) Regulations.** These may have been replaced: BoT's 2025 annual report reportedly cites "Licensing and Approval Regulations 2022", and another guide says 2021. [Unverified: the advocate should confirm which version is in force]
- **Payment Systems (Electronic Money) Regulations, 2015, GN 447** ([TanzLII](https://tanzlii.org/akn/tz/act/gn/2015/447/eng@2015-10-16)) [Official]. These are reported to require e-money to be issued only against equal funds in a trust or special account, and non-bank issuers to set up a separate trust entity ([Lexology summary](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=c9bfd8b3-c7f7-4a27-8a81-ea6d2daaf748)) [Secondary].
- **Partnering.** A BoT notice is reported to require payment system providers to get BoT approval before partnering or contracting with other entities to run payment systems ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=8f8b0d06-47f4-4ff4-a305-d3b0e807d4be)) [Secondary]. This is mainly the provider's duty, but it may slow our onboarding.
- **Aggregators exist as a licensed group.** BoT's National Payment Systems Annual Report 2025 is reported to list 72 licensed non-bank providers, including payment aggregators ([BoT report](https://www.bot.go.tz/Publications/Regular/Annual%20Report/en/2026060211494565.pdf)) [Official. I could not open the file, so the content comes from a search summary]. I found no published "marketplace" licence category.
- **Trust account guidelines.** BoT was reported to be drafting Trust Account Management Guidelines. [Unverified: I could not tell whether they are final]

### 2.2 Does Kwetuly need a licence?

I can't answer this from the sources. Here is how I frame it for the advocate:
- If **Kwetuly's own bank account** receives travellers' money, holds it until the trip or lesson, and then pays out to many guides, teachers and shops, that may look like operating a payment system or holding customer funds. A licence or BoT approval might then be needed. [Unverified]
- Two features would make it look even more like regulated activity: a stored balance or credit ("wallet") for travellers or guides, and paying out to people who did not sell anything through Kwetuly.
- The existing UI text "Held by Kwetuly" (guide dashboard, see docs/legal/README.md) should stay off until this is answered.

### 2.3 Usual structures for marketplaces (options, not recommendations)

| Option | How it works | Points for the advocate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Licensed provider holds the funds | A BoT-licensed aggregator or bank collects the card payment, keeps the funds in **its** settlement or trust account, and pays guides/teachers by mobile money on Kwetuly's instruction. Kwetuly's commission goes to Kwetuly. Kwetuly never holds traveller money in its own account. | The common structure. Does the instruction role need BoT approval? Does the provider have to approve the partnership? Who handles refunds and chargebacks? |
| B. Kwetuly is merchant of record | Kwetuly sells the lesson or trip itself and buys the service from the guide or teacher. | Simpler for payments, but Kwetuly becomes the seller. Possible TALA tour-operator licence for trips. VAT on the full price. Full consumer liability. |
| C. Commercial agent for receiving payment | Kwetuly is appointed, in writing, the guide's or teacher's agent for receiving payment. Paying Kwetuly counts as paying the guide. | Used in some other countries. **I don't know whether Tanzanian law treats this as outside payment-system licensing.** [Unverified] |
| D. Direct payment (as for items, feature c) | Traveller pays the guide or teacher directly; Kwetuly invoices its commission or fee. | No funds held. Weaker protection for travellers. Harder to collect commission. |

The payments-engineer and Walter should pick a structure only after the advocate answers. Candidate providers must be checked against BoT's published list of licensed providers. I have not named any providers because I have not checked their licences.

### 2.4 Currency points for card payments from abroad

- **Foreign Currency Usage Regulations, 2025 (GN 198 of 2025)**, in force from 28 March 2025 under the Bank of Tanzania Act. They are reported to require goods and services in Tanzania to be priced and paid in TZS, and to make advertising domestic prices in foreign currency an offence, apart from the exceptions in a Schedule ([FB Attorneys](https://fbattorneys.co.tz/foreign-exchange-regulations-issued/); [Auditax](https://auditaxinternational.co.tz/tanzania-issues-foreign-currency-usage-regulations-2025-gn-no-198-of-2025/)) [Secondary].
- Accounts of how tourism is treated conflict. A government clarification published by TATO is reported to allow foreign-currency quotes for cross-border tourism, but to require TZS for spending inside the country ([TATO](https://tatotz.org/foreign-currency-usage-regulations-in-tourism-sector/)) [Secondary].
- **What this means for Kwetuly** [Unverified]:
  - Guide and shop prices should be set and charged in TZS. Foreign-currency amounts could appear only as clearly labelled estimates, and only if the advocate agrees.
  - Online lessons for learners abroad may count as cross-border exports of services and be treated differently.
  - The localization agent should not show foreign-currency prices as the price until this is answered.

### 2.5 Other points

- **Tax.** Possible withholding on payouts (section 3.2). VAT on Kwetuly's commission. Electronic receipts (EFD/VFD) for Kwetuly's own fees. To be checked by the accounting agent and the advocate. [Unverified]
- **Refunds and chargebacks.** Card-scheme chargebacks can come up to several months after payment. We need an agreed rule on whether a payout can be held back or recovered from a guide.

## 3. Online lessons (feature a)

### 3.1 Registration or licence

- **Education law.** I found no Tanzanian rule that requires an individual private tutor teaching adults online to register. Teacher registration is reported to fall under the Education Act (No. 25 of 1978, Part VI) and its regulations, but that seems to be about schools and the teachers in them ([third-party summary](https://mabumbe.com/procedures-for-the-establishment-and-registration-of-schools/)) [Secondary]. NACTVET has a 2026 guideline on online programmes, but it covers accredited technical and vocational (TVET) institutions ([NACTVET repository](https://repository.nactvet.go.tz/items/03ad7c76-d9cd-4f07-a0e0-3e7f68e46ec4)) [Official, probably not relevant]. **Whether a platform selling lessons counts as a "school" or "tuition centre" is a question for the advocate.** [Unverified]
- **BAKITA (National Kiswahili Council).** BAKITA registers and trains Kiswahili teachers for work abroad, and runs courses on teaching Kiswahili to foreigners ([The Citizen](https://www.thecitizen.co.tz/news/1840340-5138386-8q2xdd/index.html)) [Secondary]. I found **no** rule that makes BAKITA registration compulsory for private Kiswahili teachers. [Unverified] If Kwetuly shows "BAKITA certificate" on a profile, the badge rule applies: show it only if Kwetuly has seen the certificate, and say when it was checked.
- **Online content (TCRA).** The Electronic and Postal Communications (Online Content) Regulations, 2020, as amended, are already listed as an open question in docs/legal/README.md. Live paid lessons and any recorded lessons or videos may fall within them. [Unverified]
- **Business licence and tax registration.** Teachers who earn regularly may need a TIN and possibly a business licence. Kwetuly may need a business licence for an "online education platform" activity. [Unverified]
- **Zanzibar.** Teachers living in Zanzibar may be subject to Zanzibar tax (ZRA) and licensing rules. [Unverified]

### 3.2 How teachers' income may be taxed

- **Service-fee withholding.** Sources list 5% withholding on service fees paid to residents and 15% to non-residents under section 83 of the Income Tax Act. The Finance Act 2023 amended section 83 ([PwC tax summaries](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/tanzania/corporate/withholding-taxes); [Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/a-360-second-glimpse-of-the-finance-act)) [Secondary]. Whether Kwetuly is the "payer" depends on the payment structure in section 2.3. Under Option B (merchant of record) it probably is. Under Option A or C it is less clear. [Unverified]
- **Digital content creators.** Section 83B (Finance Act 2024) is reported to require 5% withholding on payments to resident digital content creators ([TRA](https://www.tra.go.tz/page/digital-service-tax)) [Official, wording from a summary]. **Do live one-to-one lessons count as "digital content"?** Question for the advocate. [Unverified]
- **Teachers' own tax.** Individual income tax, or presumptive tax for small businesses, filed with TRA. Kwetuly should give each teacher a yearly payout statement, but it must not give tax advice.
- **Teachers outside Tanzania** ("African cooking teachers" may live in Kenya, Uganda and elsewhere). Possible 15% non-resident withholding, double-tax treaty questions, and paying out in TZS by mobile money to someone in another country. **Suggestion: start with teachers who live in Tanzania only.**
- **VAT.** Kwetuly's commission may carry VAT once Kwetuly is registered. Lessons sold to learners abroad may count as exported services. [Unverified]

### 3.3 Child safety if under-18s are allowed later

Laws that apply even now (a teacher could meet a child on a call by mistake):
- **Cybercrimes Act, 2015**, s. 13 (child pornography) ([TanzLII](https://tanzlii.org/en/akn/tz/act/2015/14/eng@2015-05-22)) [Official].
- **Child Protection Laws (Miscellaneous Amendments) Act, 2024.** Reported to amend the Cybercrimes Act and the Law of the Child Act, 2009, to use the term "child sexual abuse material" and to make online grooming a crime ([US Department of Labor country report](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ilab/resources/reports/child-labor/tanzania)) [Secondary. Unverified: exact title and sections].
- **Personal Data Protection Act, 2022.** Reported to have special rules for children's data, for example parental consent. [Unverified: section to be confirmed]
- **Learners' own countries** may have their own rules for children's data and online safety. [Unverified: not researched]

Draft safeguards if under-18s are ever allowed (for the HR and operations agents):
- Parent or guardian creates the account and gives consent.
- Teachers who teach minors pass a police clearance check, and the badge says it was checked and when.
- No private contact outside the platform.
- A parent may be present on the call.
- Clear decision, with consent, on whether calls are recorded.
- A reporting button and a written escalation procedure.
- A code of conduct each teacher signs.

**Suggestion: stay 18+.** Require learners to confirm their date of birth. Teachers' terms say they must end the lesson and report if a learner seems to be a minor.

### 3.4 Other points for lessons

- **Video provider.** If an outside video service is used, personal data goes to another country (PDPA cross-border transfer rules, see README) and the privacy policy needs updating. The default should be no recording without both sides agreeing.
- **Cooking lessons.** Teachers must not make health or nutrition claims. Allergy safety (including telling learners which ingredients a recipe uses) is the learner's responsibility, and the terms say so. Kwetuly does not sell or ship food.

### 4.3 Practical checklist for item approval (draft, for partnerships and operations agents)

1. Seller category allowed? Seller's business licence and TIN seen (record date).
2. Item on the "not allowed" list? Reject.
3. Item on the licence list? Record the licence number, the issuing body and the date seen. The listing says, for example: "Mineral dealer licence no. … seen by Kwetuly on [date]."
4. Export note on the listing, for example: "Taking this out of Tanzania may need an export permit. Ask the seller for the paperwork before you pay." Each note links only to the official authority.
5. No claims that Kwetuly cannot check ("genuine", "certified", "investment grade", "conflict-free") unless the seller provides a document and the listing names it.
6. Commission or badge fee for the seller is disclosed to travellers (Kwetuly rule).

## 5. Consumer protection and terms points for (a) to (c)

### 5.1 Laws to check (from the README, plus new ones)

- **Electronic Transactions Act, 2015 (Cap. 442 R.E. 2022), Part VI Consumer Protection, ss. 28–31.** Reported to cover: the supplier's details (full name, legal status, place of business, contacts); a chance to review and correct an order before placing it; time limits for carrying out orders; a 7-day notice cancellation right if an order is late; and a refund within 30 days if goods or services are unavailable. Exclusions are reported for financial services, auctions, and foodstuffs and beverages. ([TCRA copy of the 2015 Act](https://www.tcra.go.tz/uploads/documents/en-1619083875-The%20Electronic%20Transactions%20Act,%202015.pdf); [Ministry of Finance copy, Cap. 442 R.E. 2022](https://www.mof.go.tz/uploads/documents/en-1676545044-THE%20ELECTRONIC%20TRANSACTIONS%20ACT,%20CAP%20442%20R.E.%202022.pdf)) [Official copies. The section contents come from summaries; Unverified]
- **Fair Competition Act, 2003 (Cap. 285)** (misleading conduct, unfair terms) and the **Standard Form (Consumer Contracts) Regulations, 2014**, which may require Kwetuly's standard terms to be registered with the Fair Competition Commission. Already in the README.
- **Personal Data Protection Act, 2022**. Already in the README. New points: video lessons, teachers' ID and payout data, sellers' licence copies.

### 5.2 (a) Online lessons: terms points

- **Who is the seller of the lesson?** The teacher, or Kwetuly? This follows the payment structure (section 2.3) and decides who owes the learner a refund.
- **Booking, cancellation and no-shows** for learner and teacher, including time zones. Refunds when a teacher does not turn up. Technical failure on either side.
- **Price shown in full** before payment: lesson price, Kwetuly's fee or commission (disclosed), and currency and card conversion.
- **Age.** 18+ only, confirmed by the learner. Teacher's duty to stop and report if a learner seems to be a minor.
- **Recording.** Off by default; both sides must agree before recording.
- **Conduct.** No contact or payment outside the platform (keep it reasonable, so it is not an unfair term), no harassment, and a report button.
- **Teacher terms** (English and Kiswahili):
  - Teachers are independent, not employees. HR agent and advocate: confirm under the Employment and Labour Relations Act. [Unverified]
  - Teachers handle their own tax. Any withholding Kwetuly makes is shown on each statement.
  - Profile claims (qualifications, BAKITA, culinary school) show only what Kwetuly has checked, and when.
- **Course materials and recipes.** Who owns them, and whether Kwetuly may use clips for marketing (only with separate consent).

### 5.3 (b) Card payments and mobile-money payouts: terms points

- **Which company is paid.** The card statement name should match Kwetuly or the licensed provider, to reduce chargebacks.
- **When the guide, teacher or shop is paid** (after the service? after a dispute window?). What happens to payouts on refunds and chargebacks.
- **Currency.** The TZS price is the price (GN 198). Any foreign-currency figure is labelled as an estimate. Who bears exchange-rate and card fees.
- **Payout errors.** Wrong mobile-money number, and the payee's duty to keep their details correct.
- **No wallet or stored balance**, and no wording like "Held by Kwetuly", until BoT questions are answered.
- **Commission disclosure** on every booking (Kwetuly rule).

### 5.4 (c) Items: terms points

- **The contract is between the traveller and the shop.** Kwetuly is not the seller, does not handle payment, and is not responsible for the goods. The advocate must say how far this can go under the Fair Competition Act and the Electronic Transactions Act, given that **Kwetuly approves every item**. Approval may create a duty of care, or make Kwetuly liable for misleading listings. [Unverified]
- **What "approved" means.** One line on every listing, for example: "Approved for listing by Kwetuly on [date]: we checked [business licence / dealer licence / photos]. We did not check the quality or authenticity of the item." The approval must never be worded as "recommended" or "guaranteed".
- **Seller duties** (in the business listing terms):
  - Accurate descriptions and prices in TZS.
  - Hold every licence and permit for the item, and give buyers the export paperwork.
  - EFD/VFD receipts.
  - No goods from the not-allowed list.
  - Kwetuly may remove listings.
  - A complaint route.
- **Rentals** (gear, cars): deposit, damage, late return, safety briefing, insurance by the provider (Kwetuly does not sell or arrange insurance), and the renter's licence.
- **Restaurants.** Allergy information comes from the restaurant. Kwetuly makes no health claims.
- **Gemstones.** A special warning: "Buy only from a licensed dealer, get a receipt and the export permit/certificate of origin before you leave; stones without paperwork can be confiscated at the airport", linking to the official Mining Commission site (URL to be confirmed).
- **Complaints.** Kwetuly's role is to receive complaints, remove listings and suspend sellers. It does not refund on the shop's behalf unless Walter decides otherwise.
