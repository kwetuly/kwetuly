## 1. Lead guides reviewing crew (C4)

### 1.1 Purpose

To help good crew build a work record they can show to employers, without giving lead guides a tool to punish crew. Every rule below protects the person with less power.

### 1.2 Who can review, and when

1. Only a lead guide who was on the **same trip**, shown on the trip crew list (D1) where both the guide and the crew member were checked in.
2. Only after the trip has ended, and only within [14] days of the end.
3. One review per crew member per trip. No reviews of people who were not on the list.
4. A guide cannot review crew he is related to, or crew who work for his own business as family. **[Walter]** whether to ask guides to declare this.
5. Operators do not review individual crew through Kwetuly at launch. **[Walter]** to decide later.

### 1.3 What can be reviewed

Only **work behaviour the guide saw on that trip**, chosen from fixed tags, not free stars:

| Area | Positive tag (example) | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reliability | "On time every day" | Arrived at the gate and camps on time |
| Safe work | "Followed safety instructions" | Followed the guide's instructions on the route, in camp and in bad weather |
| Teamwork | "Helped the team" | Helped other crew, shared tasks |
| Care for travellers | "Kind and respectful to travellers" | Polite, helpful, respected privacy |
| Care for the mountain | "Kept camp clean" | Followed park rules on waste and camp sites |
| Skill (cooks) | "Good, clean cooking" | Food hygiene and meals on time |
| Skill (assistant guides) | "Clear with travellers" | Explained the route, pace and safety |

A short optional sentence may be added, limited to [200] characters, about the same work facts. The app blocks phone numbers, links and ID-like numbers in the text.

### 1.4 What must never be reviewed or used against crew

- Religion, tribe, ethnicity, sex, pregnancy, marital status, age (beyond 18 or over), disability, HIV or other health status, political views, union or association membership.
- Illness, altitude sickness or injury, or the decision to descend for health reasons.
- Weather, route closures, late park permits or anything else outside the crew member's control.
- **Refusing a load above the limit** (section 2), refusing unsafe work, or asking for food, shelter or gear the crew should have.
- **Asking about pay or tips**, complaining about late pay, or asking to see the tipping procedure.
- Reporting a problem to Kwetuly, the operator, the park, KPAP, KGA or any authority.
- Looks, accent, level of English, phone type or literacy.

A review that mentions any of these is removed, and the guide gets a reminder or warning (section 7).

### 1.5 Problems are reports, not reviews

Serious matters (theft, violence, harassment, drunkenness on duty, putting someone in danger) **do not go in a review**. The guide uses the report button. Kwetuly looks at the facts privately, tells the crew member what was reported and hears them before anything happens (section 7). Nothing is shown on the crew member's profile because of a report unless Walter decides after that process.

Smaller concerns ("was late twice") may be sent as **private feedback**: only the crew member and the Kwetuly reviewer see it. It never appears publicly and is not counted in any score.

### 1.6 What is shown, and to whom

Proposal until the advocate answers (legal note question 25):

- **No star ratings and no average score of a person.**
- The crew member's profile may show: number of trips on Kwetuly (from D1), and positive tags with how many times each was given ("Followed safety instructions: 6 trips"). Only if the crew member has switched on "Show my trip record" (section 4).
- Private feedback and reports are never public and never shared with other guides or operators.
- Before anything new appears on the profile, the crew member sees it first, in Kiswahili, and has [7] days to reply or appeal.

### 1.7 Reply and appeal

1. The crew member can reply to any review or private feedback, by app, SMS or by asking Kwetuly to write the reply for them **(to verify with tech-lead what basic phones can do)**.
2. The crew member can appeal within [14] days. The appeal is decided by a Kwetuly reviewer who was not involved. The guide does not take part in the decision.
3. While an appeal is open, the item stays hidden.
4. Outcomes: kept, edited (for example a forbidden reason removed), or removed. The crew member and the guide are told the outcome in plain words.
5. Removal of a crew member's profile, or of a guide for retaliation, is decided by Walter only. Either side may ask Walter to look again once, in writing, within [14] days.

### 1.8 No retaliation

1. No one may review, rank, refuse to hire, or report a crew member **because** they complained, refused an unsafe load, asked about pay or tips, or reported someone.
2. If a crew member has made a complaint or report about a guide or his trip, any later review or report by that guide about that crew member is **held for a human check** before the crew member sees a final version, and the check asks: is this about work facts, or about the complaint?
3. A pattern of negative feedback from one guide, much worse than other guides give the same crew member, is flagged to the reviewer.
4. Retaliation counts as serious misconduct by the guide (section 7.3).
5. Crew can report retaliation in Kiswahili, and the report is kept private from the guide where telling him would put the crew member at risk.

### 1.9 Two-way: crew tell Kwetuly how they were treated

Proposal: after each trip, every crew member on D1 can answer five private yes or no questions: paid in full; paid within [2] days; load within the limit; three meals and a tent; treated with respect. Answers are never shown one by one. A guide or operator sees only totals, and only once at least [5] crew have answered, so no single porter can be identified. Repeated "no" answers start a Kwetuly check of that operator's welfare declaration (legal note 3.4).

## 2. Porter welfare: pay and loads on Kilimanjaro

Everything in this table is **(to verify)**. None of it has been checked against a gazetted text. Kwetuly must not tell anyone "this is the law" until the advocate confirms (legal note questions 16 to 20).

| Rule | What sources say | Source and status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| National minimum wage | Labour Institutions (Minimum Wage for Private Sector) Order, GN 605A of 2025, reported in force 1 January 2026. Under "hotel and hospitality": tourist luggage porter TZS 225,000 a month or TZS 8,654 a day; tour guide TZS 320,000 a month or TZS 12,308 a day. No category found for cooks. | Law firm and wage-tracker summaries (Clyde & Co, PKF, WageIndicator), gazette not read. **Whether "tourist luggage porter" covers mountain porters is unknown.** |
| Park reference wage | USD 10 a day, linked to Government Notice No. 228 of 2009 and attributed to KINAPA/TANAPA. | News report and operator pages. GN 228 of 2009 not seen. |
| Industry wage agreement | At least TZS 25,000 a day for porters at KPAP partner companies, from a revised agreement of December 2024. A further rise in January 2026 was expected but depended on an official notice; no source found that it happened. Older figures: TZS 20,000 porters, TZS 30,000 cooks and assistant guides, TZS 40,000 lead guides a day. | KPAP and operator pages. Binding only, if at all, on companies that signed up. |
| Load limit | 20 kg of client load per porter, plus the porter's own kit, weighed at the park gate. | KPAP guidelines, operator and travel sites. Widely described as a KINAPA/TANAPA rule; no official text found. |
| Pay timing | Pay within 2 days after the descent. | KPAP, which says this is a KINAPA rule; rule not found. |
| Food, shelter and gear | Three adequate meals a day, a tent and sleeping mat, proper cold-weather gear, care and evacuation for sick or injured crew. | KPAP standards. Voluntary, not law. |
| Enforcement | KPAP and travel sites report that the wage and load rules are often ignored, with pay as low as TZS 10,000 a day reported. | Secondary reports. |
| Minimum age | Crew 18 or over (Kwetuly rule). | Legal minimum for porter work **(to verify)**, legal note question 24. |

### 2.1 Kwetuly welfare rules for anything listed on Kwetuly (proposal)

These are **Kwetuly listing rules**, not a claim about the law:

1. **Pay floor.** Every climb job post and every operator crew-welfare declaration shows a daily pay per role at or above the **Kwetuly floor** in section 5.3.
2. **Load.** No more than 20 kg of client load per porter, plus their own kit.
3. **Paid on time and traceably.** Within [2] days after the descent, by mobile money or bank.
4. **Food, tent, mat and warm gear** provided by the employer, at no cost to the crew.
5. **Sick or injured crew** are cared for and brought down, with the cost carried by the employer.
6. **Tips** go in full to the crew who were on the trip, under a procedure the traveller can see. Kwetuly takes no part of tips and does not handle them.
7. **18 or over** for all crew.
8. **No fees** charged to crew (section 3).

How each item is shown to travellers: "declared by the operator" unless Kwetuly saw evidence, and then "seen by Kwetuly on [date]". Never "porter-friendly", "ethical" or "fair" as a label (legal note 2.5). No KPAP name or logo without KPAP's written permission, which only Walter may ask for.

## 3. No fees charged to crew

### 3.1 The rule (proposal)

Nobody, on or through Kwetuly, may take money or a share from crew to give them work. That covers:

- **Kwetuly:** no sign-up fee, profile fee, job-application fee, contact-sharing fee, review fee or share of wages or tips.
- **Lead guides:** no fee to join a team (C2), to be added to a trip, to be named in a review, to get a good review, or to be recommended to an operator. No share of a porter's wage or tips. No "loan" that must be repaid from wages.
- **Operators:** no hiring fee, "registration" fee, kickback or deduction for gear, food or tents the employer must provide.
- **Third parties:** no one may sell places on a team or on a job post.

### 3.2 Gear rental

If a crew member chooses to rent extra personal gear from a shop, that is between them and the shop, at the shop's listed price. A guide or operator may not require crew to rent from a particular shop or deduct the cost from pay.

### 3.3 Reporting and consequences

Crew can report a fee demand privately in Kiswahili (report button, SMS or [CREW CONTACT]). A proven fee demand is serious misconduct (section 7.3): suspension of the guide's team features or the operator's listing, and removal on repeat. Walter decides.

### 3.4 Conflict to decide: the paid crew check (C3)

Walter set check fees on 2026-10-30 (guide US$10, cook US$7, porter US$5). The legal note (3.5) proposes that Kwetuly **never charges porters, cooks or assistant guides anything, including a badge fee**. Both cannot be true. Options for Walter:

- **(a) Recommended:** the crew check is paid by the lead guide or operator for his team, or not charged at all for porters. Crew never pay. The fee from a guide may never be passed on to the crew member (section 3.1).
- **(b)** Crew may pay for their own check, but it is always optional, never needed to be listed, named, reviewed or apply for jobs, and job posts may not require it.
- **(c)** No crew check at all until the company is registered and the advocate answers (this is already the case: nothing is charged before registration).

**[Advocate]** whether charging crew for a check could be a fee charged to job seekers under the National Employment Promotion Service Act and its 2014 regulations (legal note question 21).

## 4. Consent to be named in reviews (C5) and to be shown

### 4.1 Settings the crew member controls

Each crew member has three separate switches, all **off by default**, explained in simple Kiswahili and English:

1. **"Travellers may thank me by name in their reviews."**
2. **"Show my trip record and thank-you tags on my profile."**
3. **"Operators may see my contact details for job offers."** (C6; see section 5.6)

Each switch can be turned off at any time. Turning it off removes the name or record from public view within [48 hours].

### 4.2 How consent is given

- Given by the crew member **themselves**, from their own account, or by a one-time code sent to their own phone **(to verify with tech-lead for basic phones)**.
- A lead guide may **not** give consent for a crew member, even with their permission, and may not hold their phone or code. A team invite (C2) is accepted by the crew member, not the guide.
- Kwetuly records when and how each consent was given or withdrawn. No ID number is stored for this.
- Nobody may pressure a crew member to switch consent on, or treat them worse for leaving it off. Leaving it off has no effect on work, reviews or job applications.

### 4.3 How names appear

1. A traveller can name only crew who were on their trip, picked from the D1 crew list, to avoid wrong names. No free typing of names.
2. Only crew with switch 1 on appear in that list. Others show as "a porter", "the cook" and so on.
3. Shown as first name and an initial, with role. No surname, no photo of the crew member in a review without separate written consent, no village, phone or age.
4. **Thanks only.** A traveller review can thank a named crew member and pick a thank-you tag. It cannot give a named crew member stars or a negative remark. A traveller who has a complaint about a crew member uses the report route, and the complaint goes to the operator and Kwetuly privately.
5. If a review that names a crew member is otherwise negative (for example about the operator), the name is still only used in a thank-you line, never in the negative part.
6. If a name was attached wrongly, the crew member or guide can ask for it to be removed, and Kwetuly removes it without asking for proof.

### 4.4 Photos and stories

Any photo, video or story about a crew member in Kwetuly marketing or social media needs their separate written consent for that use, can be withdrawn, and must follow the voice guide (no pity wording). Health, family hardship or money needs are never shared without specific consent (ideas I3).

## 5. Job posts (C6)

### 5.1 Who may post

- Licensed operators with a recorded licence of the right class (legal note 2.3), and lead guides with an approved profile and a recorded registration check.
- **Climb jobs** (Kilimanjaro, Meru and similar) only from operators with a mountain-climbing licence, or a lead guide posting for such an operator, naming it.
- No posts from sample accounts (`is_sample`), and no posts by Kwetuly on behalf of anyone.

### 5.2 Required fields (the post cannot be sent without them)

1. Employer name (the operator or guide who will pay) and, for climbs, the operator's licence as recorded.
2. Role and number of people (for example 5 porters, 1 cook).
3. Route or work area, start date and number of days.
4. **Pay per day in TZS, before tips**, per role. "Negotiable", "tips only", "to be discussed" and ranges that start below the floor are not accepted.
5. When pay is made (within [2] days after the end) and how (mobile money or bank).
6. What the employer provides: meals, tent, sleeping mat, warm gear, transport to the gate.
7. Load limit for porters (no more than 20 kg of client load).
8. How tips are shared.
9. Type of engagement as the employer states it (for example "per climb"). **[Advocate]** whether a "per climb" engagement is a specific-task employment contract (legal note question 27). Kwetuly does not choose this label for the employer.
10. Confirmation tick: "I charge no fee to anyone who applies or is hired, and I will pay at least the amount shown."

### 5.3 The pay floor

Walter's rule is "at least the government minimum wage". Because the legal figure for mountain crew is unclear (section 2), the form needs one number per role. Proposal for Walter:

| Role | Proposed Kwetuly floor per day (gross, before tips) | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Porter | TZS [25,000] | Highest of: GN 605A porter daily rate (TZS 8,654, if it applies), USD 10 park reference, December 2024 industry agreement (TZS 25,000). All to verify |
| Cook | TZS [30,000] | Older industry figure; no legal category found |
| Assistant guide | TZS [30,000] | Older industry figure; must be at least the tour guide legal day rate (TZS 12,308, to verify) |
| Lead guide | TZS [40,000] | Older industry figure; at least TZS 12,308 (to verify) |
| Driver | TZS [Walter to set] | No figure researched; transfers are off |

- The floor is written on the site as **"Kwetuly's minimum for posts on Kwetuly"**, never as "the legal minimum wage", until the advocate confirms the legal figure.
- If the legal minimum turns out to be higher than a floor above, the legal figure wins at once.
- Floors are reviewed every [6] months and whenever a new wage order or park notice is confirmed. Walter approves each change.
- The form refuses a pay figure below the floor and shows the floor in the message.
- **[Advocate]** whether Kwetuly setting a pay floor for listings could point to Kwetuly as an employer under s. 61 of the Labour Institutions Act. My reading (not legal advice) is that a listing rule is not control of the work or hours, but it must be checked.

### 5.4 Posts that are refused

- Pay below the floor, unpaid "trial" climbs, pay only in tips, or pay held until the "season ends".
- Any fee, deposit or "registration" asked from applicants.
- Preferences about religion, tribe, sex, pregnancy, marital status, HIV or other health status, or age beyond 18 or over. **[Advocate]** whether any genuine job requirement could ever allow a sex-specific post (for example a female assistant guide for a women-only group); refused until answered.
- Under-18 workers.
- Non-citizen guides or assistant guides (GN 487A, to verify).
- Requirements to bring one's own tent or food for a climb.
- Requirements to pay for a Kwetuly crew check (section 3.4).

### 5.5 Checks against fake jobs

- Every post is looked at by a Kwetuly reviewer before it is shown, at least at launch.
- Posts end on the start date and are removed automatically.
- Crew can report a post. Three reports pause it until checked.
- The site tells crew in Kiswahili: "Kwetuly will never ask you for money to apply. If anyone asks, report it."
- After the job, crew on D1 can answer the private welfare questions (section 1.9). Paid less than the post said is treated as a serious report.

### 5.6 Crew contact details

- Walter decided operators pay to see crew contact details. Crew are never charged.
- Only crew with switch 3 on (section 4.1) are shown, and only to verified operators.
- Each contact view is logged. Crew can see which operators viewed their details and can block an operator.
- Operators may use the details only to offer work. No resale, marketing lists or sharing.
- **[Advocate]** whether charging operators for contact details makes Kwetuly a private employment promotion agency needing approval (legal note question 28), and the PDPA basis for sharing.

## 6. Short crew code of conduct

For crew and for lead guides who lead crew. Accepted once in the app, in Kiswahili or English, read aloud on request **(to verify how on basic phones)**.

### Part A – English

**For all crew**
1. Be on time, follow the lead guide's safety instructions and the park rules.
2. Treat travellers, other crew and local people with respect. No harassment, no sexual comments or contact, no insults or discrimination.
3. No alcohol or drugs while working.
4. Respect travellers' privacy and belongings. Do not take photos of travellers without asking.
5. Keep the mountain and camps clean. Carry waste down as the park requires.
6. Tell the lead guide at once if you or anyone else feels unwell.
7. Do not ask travellers for tips, gifts or money beyond the agreed tipping procedure.
8. Do not pretend to have been on a trip you were not on, and do not take tips meant for others.

**For lead guides leading crew**
1. Make sure every crew member gets the pay, food, tent, gear and load limit the job promised.
2. Never charge crew any fee or take any share of their pay or tips.
3. Never hold a crew member's phone, ID document or consent code.
4. Give feedback only about work on the trip, using the review rules (section 1). Never punish a crew member for asking about pay, refusing an unsafe load, being ill or reporting a problem.
5. Look after sick or injured crew as you would a traveller.

**Your rights**
- You may refuse a load above 20 kg of client load, or work that is unsafe, without any effect on your reviews.
- You may report any problem privately, in Kiswahili, to [CREW CONTACT] or with the report button. Reporting in good faith is never punished.
- Kwetuly never asks you for money.

### Part B – Kiswahili (rasimu; inahitaji kukaguliwa na mzungumzaji wa Kiswahili cha asili)

**Kwa wafanyakazi wote wa safari (wapagazi, wapishi, wasaidizi wa waongoza watalii, madereva)**
1. Fika kwa wakati, fuata maelekezo ya usalama ya mwongoza watalii mkuu na kanuni za hifadhi.
2. Waheshimu watalii, wafanyakazi wenzako na wenyeji. Hakuna unyanyasaji, maneno au kuguswa kwa kingono, matusi wala ubaguzi.
3. Usinywe pombe wala kutumia dawa za kulevya ukiwa kazini.
4. Heshimu faragha na mali za watalii. Usiwapige picha watalii bila kuwaomba.
5. Weka mlima na kambi safi. Shusha taka kama hifadhi inavyotaka.
6. Mjulishe mwongoza watalii mkuu mara moja wewe au mtu mwingine akijisikia vibaya.
7. Usiwaombe watalii bakshishi, zawadi au pesa nje ya utaratibu wa bakshishi uliokubaliwa.
8. Usijifanye ulikuwa kwenye safari ambayo hukuwepo, na usichukue bakshishi ya wengine.

**Kwa waongoza watalii wakuu wanaoongoza wafanyakazi**
1. Hakikisha kila mfanyakazi anapata malipo, chakula, hema, vifaa na kikomo cha mzigo kilichoahidiwa kazini.
2. Usimtoze mfanyakazi ada yoyote wala kuchukua sehemu ya malipo au bakshishi yake.
3. Usishike simu, kitambulisho au namba ya ridhaa ya mfanyakazi.
4. Toa maoni kuhusu kazi ya safari tu, kwa kufuata kanuni za maoni (sehemu ya 1). Usimwadhibu mfanyakazi kwa kuuliza kuhusu malipo, kukataa mzigo usio salama, kuugua au kuripoti tatizo.
5. Mtunze mfanyakazi aliyeugua au kuumia kama ungemtunza mtalii.

**Haki zako**
- Unaweza kukataa mzigo wa mtalii unaozidi kilo 20, au kazi isiyo salama, bila athari yoyote kwa maoni yako.
- Unaweza kuripoti tatizo lolote kwa siri, kwa Kiswahili, kwa [MAWASILIANO YA WAFANYAKAZI] au kwa kitufe cha kuripoti. Kuripoti kwa nia njema hakuadhibiwi kamwe.
- Kwetuly haikuombi pesa kamwe.

## 7. Steps when rules are broken

Same steps as the teacher code (section 11), adjusted for crew:

7.1 Kwetuly looks at the facts, tells the person what was reported (without exposing a reporter who could be put at risk) and hears them, in Kiswahili, before deciding, unless there is an urgent safety risk.

7.2 Steps: (1) reminder or written warning; (2) hide a job post, team feature or profile item until fixed; (3) suspend while Kwetuly looks into a serious report; (4) remove from Kwetuly.

7.3 **Serious misconduct** (step 3 at once): violence, threats or sexual harassment; charging crew fees or taking their pay or tips; retaliation against crew; posting jobs below the floor or not paying what a post promised; using under-18 crew; false documents.

7.4 Walter makes every suspension and removal decision. Records are private, with no more personal data than needed. Appeal to Walter once, in writing, within [14] days.

7.5 Kwetuly does not remove a crew member's work record because a guide or operator asks, only after the process above.

## 8. Internal notes for the reviewer (not for crew)

- Record outcomes in the admin tools, never in shared documents, chats or git.
- Never ask crew about religion, tribe, pregnancy, marital status, HIV or other health status, political views or union membership, in vetting, appeals or welfare questions.
- Many crew use basic phones and some read little: offer to read texts aloud by phone call, and never treat slow replies as a lack of interest.
- Decisions about a crew member's work record are made by a person, never automatically by a score.
