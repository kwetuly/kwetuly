# Kwetuly voice

Status: draft by marketing, 2026-11-08. Walter approves it. Walter also checks all Kiswahili wording before anyone uses it.

Walter, 2026-11-08: "We Tanzanians are polite, so let's be polite." Kwetuly is about local people. Every line should first sound natural and courteous to a Tanzanian reader, and then sound warm to a visitor.

## 1. Who we speak about, in this order

1. **Local guides.** Guides are the way in. A traveller meets and chooses a guide first, and the rest of the trip follows from that person. Our promise stays the same: "Tanzania is only as good as your guide. So choose yours."
2. **Porters and cooks.** Porters and cooks make the mountain and the camp possible. Name them with respect and never as extras. Walter says "wagumu" for porters, and the usual written word is "wapagazi". Walter chooses which one we use in public texts.
3. **Drivers.** Drivers are local people we are proud of. **Transfers are switched off**, so we name drivers as people, not as a service to book. Do not write "book a driver", "airport transfer" or "match-day transfers" until Walter switches transfers on.
4. **Hosts and owners.** This means owners of hotels, lodges, homestays and Airbnb-style apartments, and owners of curio, gem and gear-rental shops. Walter's wording is "Airbnb-style apartments", never "Airbnb" alone. These owners list with us. A "Checked by Kwetuly" badge says what was checked and when. It never says "recommended".
5. **Other local people.** This means people who cook at home, craftspeople, families, neighbours and the people of the place a traveller visits. We speak about them as hosts, not as sights.

The traveller is always a guest (mgeni). Local people are always people with names, skills and their own voice. In traveller texts, never call them "staff", "crew to hire" or "authentic locals".

## 2. Politeness rules

1. **Greet first.** Start a page, an email or a message with a greeting such as "Karibu", "Habari" or "Hello". Do not start with a demand or a form.
2. **Say Karibu.** Use "Karibu" for one person and "Karibuni" for more than one. It is our welcome word in every language, and it stays in Kiswahili.
3. **Say please and thank you.** Every request says "please" / "tafadhali". Every finished action says "thank you" / "asante". When the fault may be ours, the error message says "sorry" / "samahani".
4. **Invite rather than order.** Prefer "You are welcome to...", "Would you like to...", "Karibu..." or "Unakaribishwa..." over bare commands. Button labels can stay short ("Send", "Tuma"), but the line above the button invites.
5. **No hype.** Do not use "best", "No. 1", "top-rated", "unbeatable", "amazing", "once in a lifetime", "authentic" or "hidden gem". Use exclamation marks only after "Karibu" or "Asante". No all caps. No arrows on buttons.
6. **Never claim a service that is switched off.** Today that includes transfers. The soft-launch plan has deposits off, so check before any line says "pay", "deposit" or "safe payment". If something is coming soon, say so plainly, and do not give a date we cannot keep.
7. **Never invent.** Do not use numbers, reviews, ratings, partners, awards or quotes that are not real. Always label sample guides and listings as samples.
8. **Respect people and places.** No pity, and no words like "poor" or "primitive". Do not use "tribe" as decoration. Do not use photos or words that treat people as scenery. Say who someone is and what they do.
9. **Use plain words.** Write short sentences, with one idea per line. Explain checks honestly, for example "ID and licence checked" and "Stated by the guide".
10. **Respect elders and titles.** In direct Kiswahili messages, use "ndugu", "mama" or "mzee" where it fits. In formal texts, do not call older owners by first name alone unless they chose it.

## 5. Politeness in each language

By localization, 2026-11-08, short version of Section 2 of `docs/brand/voice-politeness-notes.md` (full detail and open decisions there). Draft until Walter approves. Everywhere: short buttons may be plain commands; headings, body text, errors and anything asking for personal data or money are polite requests. "Karibu" stays in Kiswahili and opens the sentence ("Karibu! Welcome to...").

**English.** Neutral "you". "Please" in requests, "Thank you" after actions, "Sorry" when the fault may be ours. Buttons: plain verbs ("Send", "Sign in").

**Kiswahili (Tanzania).** Walter checks every line.
- Welcome: "Karibu" to one person (all screens), "Karibuni" only to a group (group trip chat, banners, posts to everyone). "Karibu sana" is warmer. Thanks: "Asante" (one), "Asanteni" (many); pick "Asante" or "Ahsante" and keep it. No "Shikamoo" in the app (we do not know the user's age); "Habari" or "Karibu" are safe.
- Thank after an action: "Asante! Ombi lako limetumwa." "Samahani" before errors and refusals; "Pole" for sympathy ("Pole kwa usumbufu").
- Buttons may be plain (Ingia, Tuma, Endelea, Hifadhi). Elsewhere ask: "Tafadhali weka namba yako", "Karibu umchague", "Ungependa...?", "Tunaomba uthibitishe barua pepe yako". No chains of bare commands; add "Karibu" or "kisha". Avoid "lazima" and "unapaswa"; use "tafadhali hakikisha".
- Address: singular (wewe, -ako, u-) by default. Plural (ninyi, -enu, -ni) only for a whole group, and never mixed in one sentence ("Safirini pamoja, endeleeni kuwasiliana"). "Mkubaliane", "mpange" (you and the guide) is correct and treats the guide as an equal.
- Tanzanian words: "namba" (not "nambari"), "barua pepe", "pesa ya simu", "mwongozaji/waongozaji". No Sheng. Speak of guides, drivers and hosts with respect ("waongozaji wa ndani", "wenyeji").

**French (vous).** Keep vous throughout. Buttons in the infinitive ("Se connecter", "Envoyer"). Requests: "Veuillez..." or "Merci de...". Space before ! ? : ; ("Bienvenue sur Kwetuly !").

**German (Sie).** Keep Sie, always capitalised (Sie, Ihr, Ihnen). Buttons in the infinitive ("Anmelden", "Senden"). "Bitte" in requests and errors ("Bitte prüfen Sie...").

**Italian (tu).** Keep the warm tu usual in Italian travel apps; "Lei" sounds like a bank letter. "Per favore" in errors, "grazie" after actions. Buttons in the tu imperative ("Accedi", "Invia"). Welcome without assuming gender: "Ti diamo il benvenuto su Kwetuly!" rather than "Benvenuto" (Walter decides).

**Polish (Ty, capitalised).** Ty forms with capitalised pronouns (Ty, Tobie, Cię); Pan/Pani is avoided because we do not know the user's gender. Welcome: "Witamy w Kwetuly!". Buttons in the imperative ("Zaloguj się", "Wyślij").

**Chinese (你 or 您).** Today traveller texts use 你 and only legal texts use 您. 您 is the usual polite form in Chinese travel and booking services; Walter decides whether to switch all traveller texts at once. Never mix the two. Use 请 for requests, 谢谢 after actions, 欢迎 for welcome ("欢迎来到 Kwetuly！").
